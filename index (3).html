OUR STORY

Three decades in the same market

Toby Simmonds set up the first olive stall in Ireland right here in the English Market — long before anyone in Cork knew what a Nocellara was. Three decades on, the stall is still exactly where you'd expect to find it: between the fishmonger and the cheese counter, smelling faintly of garlic and lemon.

1993

Toby Simmonds opens the first olive stall in Ireland, right here in the English Market.

2009

Toby imports Ireland's first water buffalo, chasing the idea of real buffalo mozzarella.

Today

Over 15 cheeses now come out of the Toonsbridge creamery, sold right alongside the olives that started it all.

HOW WE CURE

From tree to jar

Every olive goes through the same four stages before it reaches the stall — none of it fast, all of it worth the wait.

1

Harvest

Olives are picked by hand at exactly the right ripeness — too early and they're bitter, too late and they lose their bite.

2

Brine

Weeks in a salt-water bath draw out the natural bitterness that makes a raw olive inedible.

3

Marinate

Herbs, citrus, chilli or garlic go in next — this is where each jar gets its personality.

4

Jar & taste

Packed into brine or oil and put out on the counter, ready for someone to ask "can I try that one?"
